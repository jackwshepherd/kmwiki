# Transaction Management

**Summary**: Transaction management tools shift legal deal work from unstructured to structured processes — and in doing so, create structured data and natural nudges to knowledge sharing that unstructured (Word-based) approaches cannot.

## The Unstructured Problem

Since email arrived, transactions have been managed largely the same way: conference calls for weekly updates, emails to exchange drafts, Word tables or spreadsheets for the closing checklist. These processes grew organically. Outputs are not captured. No two transactions are managed identically. The data produced — who signed what, how many documents were exchanged, how long each stage took — is trapped inside Word tables that no system can read.

This is **unstructured**: meaningful information exists, but not in machine-readable form.

---

## What Structured Transaction Management Adds

Transaction management tools shift from unstructured to structured processes by labelling everything.

**In a Word checklist:** A row is just a row. A column saying "Signatory: J. Smith" is just text.

**In a transaction management tool:** Creating a row creates a *document*. Adding a signatory creates a *signatory record*. Every piece of information is a data point.

This unlocks basic questions that were previously unanswerable without human intervention:
- How many documents were signed?
- How many drafts did we exchange of the Share Purchase Agreement?
- How does this transaction compare to the last ten similar ones?

It also enables combining data: a checklist row linked to the actual document file means every version addition is logged. You can pinpoint exactly how many drafts were exchanged because the checklist status and the document file are connected.

## The KM Connection

Transaction management and knowledge management have a missing link: **structured deal data is structured knowledge data**.

The deal bible — the collection of executed documents at the end of a transaction — is a knowledge asset. With unstructured ways of working, it gets lost. People finish the deal, celebrate, move to the next matter, and forget to share anything.

Transaction management tools change this because:

1. **Natural nudges** — the deal bible creation process within the tool is a structured moment where lawyers are already doing something they have to do. Knowledge sharing can be woven into that existing task rather than added as an extra burden.

2. **Automation potential** — the process of sharing executed documents with colleagues can be automated or made seamless, rather than depending on someone remembering to do it after a 48-hour closing stretch.

3. **Structured data for AI** — structured transaction data gives AI systems far more to work with. Questions like "how did we handle this clause across our last 50 deals?" become answerable. With Word-based processes, they're not.

> *In the future, these tools may be adopted primarily for their knowledge sharing capabilities, rather than purely as collaboration and efficiency tools.*

## The Pandemic Catalyst

Hybrid working forced lawyers to find new ways to structure work. Several unintended positive effects:

- **E-signatures** — adopted out of necessity (no printers at home), discovered to be more efficient
- **Video recording** — corridor meetings became Zoom calls; those calls got recorded; transcripts became available later
- **Teams** — training sessions stored for later reference

Transaction management tools rode this wave of positive experience with structured ways of working. Lawyers who had learned that structured tools worked better in a hybrid setting were more open to structured transaction management.

The pandemic-driven shift from corridor conversations to recorded calls also created a form of structured knowledge capture almost by accident — conversations that would previously have been lost were now logged.

---

## The Broader Point

Structured processes are a prerequisite for both good knowledge management and good AI deployment. A process that produces consistent, labelled, machine-readable data points enables:

- Comparison across matters
- Automatic knowledge capture at natural moments
- AI queries that return meaningful answers
- Metrics that prove value (time to close, document exchange counts, etc.)

Unstructured processes produce none of these. This is why structuring how lawyers work — before asking AI to work on it — is foundational to almost everything else.
